### **Interview 2: SUR-2 (product management, RCA)**

I2-1 **[Suraj]** Thanks, SUR-2. I've got about six minutes to understand your placement preparation. Can I record this for the course assignment, with access limited to the course team?

I2-2 **[SUR-2]** Yes, that's okay. I may need to leave when my group calls, but we have a few minutes.

I2-3 **[Suraj]** What roles are you working towards?

I2-4 **[SUR-2]** Product management. Um, yeah. I was a software developer in Bengaluru for three years before ISB. I'm practising... product sense, root cause analysis, and the usual personal questions. The product questions are new for me. The personal questions should be easier, but sometimes those also become technical explanations. **J**

I2-5 **[Suraj]** Tell me about the last practice you did.

I2-6 **[SUR-2]** Last night, actually. Yeah, last night. One of my friends asked why a payments app's transactions had dropped. I started... just listing possible reasons. App crash, festival season, bank issue... I had a lot of reasons. Then he asked which metric had dropped, and I realised... uh, I hadn't clarified that. I knew the framework from a video, but I hadn't used it properly in the conversation. **P**

I2-7 **[Suraj]** Okay. And then? What happened after he asked that?

I2-8 **[SUR-2]** We stopped. Restarted, basically. I asked whether it was transaction count or value, whether all users were affected, and when it started. The second attempt was better, but, um, I had already heard part of the case. So I couldn't really... count that as improvement. I needed a fresh question. We didn't have time because he had another mock after mine. **J P G**

I2-9 **[Suraj]** How had you prepared before that session?

I2-10 **[SUR-2]** Um... about twenty minutes reading an RCA framework and two solved examples. Then I wrote headings in Notion. I do that a lot. Collect things, I mean. When I open the page it looks very organised. But that doesn't mean I can answer when somebody changes the question. Last week I had... three mocks. Two were mostly product cases; one was behavioural. **J P**

I2-11 **[Suraj]** What happened in the behavioural one?

I2-12 **[SUR-2]** He asked about disagreeing with a stakeholder. I spoke about a feature our engineering team delayed. I explained the latency issue in detail. He said he understood the technical problem but not why I made that decision for the customer. I'd assumed that part was... obvious. It was obvious to me because I worked on it for months. To him it was just some backend thing. **P**

#### I2-13 **[Suraj]** Mm. What did you change?

I2-14 **[SUR-2]** I added the customer complaint that started the discussion. Then I explained... no, first I explained the option I recommended and what we gave up. I didn't actually own the roadmap, so I was careful about that. I influenced a release decision; I wasn't the product manager. The first rewrite made me sound as if I'd owned everything, which wasn't true. **J G Q**

I2-15 **[Suraj]** Where did that rewrite come from?

I2-16 **[SUR-2]** ChatGPT, yeah. I asked it to make the answer more suitable for a PM interview. The wording sounded impressive, but it moved from what I did to what a product manager would ideally do. So, uh, I took out those parts. I still use it to ask follow-up questions, though. It's available at midnight when my friends are asleep. That is genuinely useful. **J P Q**

I2-17 **[Suraj]** How do you decide which feedback to act on?

I2-18 **[SUR-2]** If someone points to an actual moment, I can work with it. Like... you gave five causes before asking what changed. That's clear. If it just says improve structure, I don't know where to... where do I start? With the stakeholder answer, my friend asked what the customer would have noticed if we had shipped. I could answer that. So I put that earlier in the story. **G Q**

I2-19 **[Suraj]** Did you test that version again?

I2-20 **[SUR-2]** Yes. With an alum, on a call. He followed the story, but asked how I knew the complaints were representative. I... didn't have a strong answer. That was useful too. Better wording hadn't fixed the missing evidence. I went back to an old presentation and checked what we had actually measured. There were... fewer data points than I remembered. **P Q**

I2-21 **[Suraj]** And, uh, what did that extra preparation cost you?

I2-22 **[SUR-2]** Oh... around an hour finding the presentation and changing my notes. Not all wasted, because now I know what I can claim. But I had planned to do a product design question that evening. I keep switching between learning cases and fixing stories. They need different... different types of feedback, and I put them in the same prep slot. **P**

I2-23 **[Suraj]** What tells you a practice session went well?

I2-24 **[SUR-2]** A new question where I remember to clarify before jumping in. Or somebody can repeat my reasoning back without me adding another explanation. I don't need every answer to be exactly ninety seconds. Sometimes the interviewer wants detail, right? I want to notice when I'm giving detail they didn't ask for. **G**

I2-25 **[Suraj]** Anything I missed before you go?

I2-26 **[SUR-2]** Um, just that I like my peer mocks. Scheduling is annoying, but I wouldn't replace all of them. The unexpected follow-up is the valuable bit. We normally have to get back to group work after the mock. **G**

I2-27 **[Suraj]** You mentioned collecting things in Notion. What did you actually open before yesterday's mock?

I2-28 **[SUR-2]** The framework page. Not the feedback from the last session, which... yeah, that would've been more useful. The feedback was in a different page. I'd written, clarify the metric, right at the top. So I had the note. I just didn't... use it. After the mock I moved that line beside the question I was practising. Only

that line, not the whole feedback document. Otherwise I'll spend twenty minutes organising it and call that preparation. I've done that also. **J P**

I2-29 **[Suraj]** When have you managed to use a note during a fresh attempt?

I2-30 **[SUR-2]** In the behavioural mock last week. Before starting, I looked at a reminder to explain the customer impact. I didn't read the answer. Just that reminder. Then when my friend looked confused, I stopped and asked which part wasn't clear. Normally I keep talking, hoping the next sentence will explain it. This time she said she didn't understand the trade-off. So we stayed there and discussed it. That was better than reaching the end of a very complete answer that she hadn't followed. **J G**

I2-31 **[Suraj]** Got it. Thanks, I'll let you get back to your group.

I2-32 **[SUR-2]** Yeah, no problem. Oh, they're calling now. Thanks.
