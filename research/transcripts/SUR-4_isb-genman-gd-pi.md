### **Interview 4: SUR-4 (group discussion and personal interview)**

I4-1 **[Suraj]** Thanks, SUR-4. I'm asking classmates about placement preparation. We have about six minutes. Can I record this for the assignment, with access only for the course team?

I4-2 **[SUR-4]** Yeah, sure. I have a meeting later, but six minutes is fine.

I4-3 **[Suraj]** What have you been practising recently?

I4-4 **[SUR-4]** Um, general management processes, especially group discussions and personal interviews. I worked in consumer sales in Delhi for three years before ISB. I speak to people quite easily one to one. In a group discussion, with everybody trying to enter, it's a... different thing. I don't think my problem is knowing English or not having opinions. **J**

I4-5 **[Suraj]** Walk me through your last group practice.

I4-6 **[SUR-4]** Saturday... yeah, we did one on Saturday with six people and an observer. The topic was about quick commerce and neighbourhood shops. I had... two points ready, but somebody started before me. Then two people were arguing about employment. I kept waiting for a clean gap. By the time I spoke, my first point had already been made. So I... repeated it, just with a different example. **P**

I4-7 **[Suraj]** Mm-hm. What did you do next?

I4-8 **[SUR-4]** I tried to... uh, bring in something from my sales experience, about retailers carrying inventory. But I explained the background for too long. Someone interrupted. And I... let it go. After that I mostly nodded. In the last minute I gave a summary, which was okay, actually. The observer said my summary added value, but I had been quiet for most of the discussion. **P G**

I4-9 **[Suraj]** How had you prepared for that session?

I4-10 **[SUR-4]** Read a few articles... watched two discussions online, and wrote some opening lines. Forty... maybe forty minutes. That preparation wasn't completely wasted; I understood the topic. But I'd practised opening an empty room, basically. In the actual group, there was already a conversation going on. My prepared opening had nowhere to go. **J P Q**

I4-11 **[Suraj]** What did you try after the feedback?

I4-12 **[SUR-4]** We ran a second round. So I thought, okay, just... speak. I told myself to speak early. Then I came in over somebody, and it sounded... rude to me. The observer said the entry was okay, but I had immediately said sorry and stopped. So I wasn't sure what to change. One person said be more assertive. Another said don't look aggressive. Both instructions are in my head now. **P**

I4-13 **[Suraj]** What made either piece of feedback useful or unhelpful?

I4-14 **[SUR-4]** The observer remembered the actual... moment: I said sorry before I'd even finished the first sentence. That I could understand. Just saying assertive doesn't tell me much. We didn't record the group, so there was no way to replay it. I remember feeling that I interrupted badly. Maybe it wasn't that bad. I don't know. I'm relying on... everyone's memory. **P G**

I4-15 **[Suraj]** Okay. How often have you done this kind of practice?

I4-16 **[SUR-4]** Twice. In the past two weeks. Getting six people free is the main issue. That Saturday we spent fifteen minutes waiting for everybody, then, um, about thirty-five on both rounds and feedback. On my own I record answers sometimes, but that doesn't teach me when to enter a group. There isn't another person carrying on while I'm thinking. **J P**

I4-17 **[Suraj]** Tell me about the personal interview practice you mentioned.

I4-18 **[SUR-4]** A friend asked why I wanted general management after sales. My first answer was... something like wanting a broader business perspective. Very standard. She asked what I had actually done to explore other functions. Then I remembered... working with supply planning during a stockout. That example made the answer more specific. We spent only ten minutes on it, but it helped. **J G**

I4-19 **[Suraj]** What changed when you tried it again?

I4-20 **[SUR-4]** I could explain why the supply decision interested me, instead of listing functions I wanted exposure to. I still went a little long, though. My friend asked me to remove a sentence and try again. That kind of immediate retry works for me. For group discussions, though, I need a group. A cleaner personal answer isn't the same as getting comfortable entering a live discussion. **G P Q**

I4-21 **[Suraj]** What tools or materials are you using?

I4-22 **[SUR-4]** WhatsApp to organise people... articles for topics, a notebook, phone recordings for personal answers. Sometimes ChatGPT gives me topics and counterarguments. I don't use its full speeches. I tried... reading one aloud and it was... too formal. Nobody in our practice group was speaking like that. Also, knowing a great counterargument didn't mean I managed to say it at the right... time. **J P**

I4-23 **[Suraj]** What would count as progress in your next session?

I4-24 **[SUR-4]** Entering when I have something useful, and finishing a short point without apologising unnecessarily. Not just speaking more seconds. In the second round I spoke more, but I repeated someone else's point once. That doesn't feel like progress. I'd like the observer to track whether I added something, or helped the group move, rather than only counting entries. **J G**

I4-25 **[Suraj]** Anything else I should understand?

I4-26 **[SUR-4]** The group itself matters. Um... With friends who leave space, I'm fine. With two very fast speakers, I disappear. Please don't turn that into, SUR-4 needs better content. Content is one part, but it wasn't the main thing happening on Saturday. **P Q**

I4-27 **[Suraj]** In the round where you summarised, how did you find a way into the conversation?

I4-28 **[SUR-4]** Someone asked how much time was left. That gave me an opening. I said, we have two different points here, and tried to connect them. No special line I'd memorised. That came quite naturally, actually. I was listening because I hadn't been speaking. But I don't want to wait till the end every time. Also, I left out one person's point in the summary. She mentioned it afterwards. So it wasn't... perfect. Still, it was the part where I felt I was helping the group rather than fighting for a turn. **J P G**

I4-29 **[Suraj]** What did you do with that feedback afterwards?

I4-30 **[SUR-4]** Wrote down what I'd missed, and asked her how she would've connected it. We discussed it for two minutes while leaving. She didn't give me a whole speech, just the link between her point and the employment discussion. That helped. I've kept it in the notebook with the topic. I haven't tested it in another group yet. We planned one, but two people couldn't make it. I read the notes again on my own. Useful, maybe, but I can't claim I've fixed the entry problem from reading them. **J P**

I4-31 **[Suraj]** Mm, understood. Thanks for making time.

I4-32 **[SUR-4]** Sure, happy to help. Let me know if you speak to the observer also.
