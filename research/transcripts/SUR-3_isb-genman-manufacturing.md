### **Interview 3: SUR-3 (general management)**

I3-1 **[Suraj]** Thanks for making time. This is about how you're preparing for placements. We have roughly six minutes. Is recording okay for the assignment, shared only with the course team?

I3-2 **[SUR-3]** Yeah, okay. Please leave client names out. Some of the examples are from work.

I3-3 **[Suraj]** Of course. What roles are you preparing for?

I3-4 **[SUR-3]** General management and, uh... leadership programmes, mainly. Before ISB I spent five years in manufacturing operations, around Pune. I can discuss the plant work comfortably with an operations person. With someone from another function, I sometimes go too far into the process. Then the answer has finished, but my point hasn't come out. **J P**

- I3-5 **[Suraj]** When did that happen most recently?

I3-6 **[SUR-3]** Let me think... Sunday. In an alumni mock. He asked about handling conflict. I described a maintenance shutdown where production and maintenance had different priorities. I talked about... the machines, the shift plan, the safety checks. He stopped me and asked who I had disagreed with. I was already, uh... two or three minutes in. That should have been the first thing I explained, probably. **P**

- I3-7 **[Suraj]** Mm. What had you been trying to explain?

I3-8 **[SUR-3]** Why the shutdown mattered. That was... yeah, that was what I wanted to explain. If you don't know the plant, the risk doesn't sound serious. But I went into too much detail. The actual conflict was that the production supervisor didn't want to lose another shift. I had to... propose a smaller trial shutdown and get both sides to agree. That conversation was the story. I hardly spoke about it. **P**

I3-9 **[Suraj]** What feedback did the alum give?

I3-10 **[SUR-3]** He said... explain it as if I'm from sales. That helped. Then he asked what I personally changed, because I kept saying our team. See, I don't like taking credit for other people's work. There were technicians with much more experience than me. But in trying to be fair, I made it sound like I was just standing nearby. **P Q**

I3-11 **[Suraj]** What did you do with that feedback?

I3-12 **[SUR-3]** I made two columns on paper, team actions and my actions. My side had... the trial plan, the meeting with the supervisor, and the escalation call. The team side had the repair work and restart checks. That was easier than... um, rewriting the full answer immediately. Later I said it aloud to my roommate, who has a finance background. **J G**

I3-13 **[Suraj]** What happened with your roommate?

I3-14 **[SUR-3]** He understood the disagreement. He still didn't understand two technical words, so I changed those. We spent... twenty minutes, maybe. The alumni session had been forty minutes. Then another... half hour on my own notes. I don't do that every day. This month I've had two proper mocks; most preparation is after dinner, between other work. Getting the same alum again is... difficult. **P G**

I3-15 **[Suraj]** What other preparation have you tried?

I3-16 **[SUR-3]** Uh, the career team material, seniors' question lists, and a spreadsheet of examples. I tried asking an AI tool to rewrite one story. I removed the company name first. It gave me a neat answer, but added that I led the entire initiative. I hadn't. It also turned an estimate into an exact saving. I closed it and went back to my own notes. **J P**

I3-17 **[Suraj]** Was anything... from that attempt useful?

I3-18 **[SUR-3]** The order, actually. Yeah. Situation, decision, result. That was useful. I don't think the tool was useless; I asked a broad question and got a broad answer. But I couldn't give it the full incident report because it contains things I shouldn't share. So... the context was incomplete too. I was not comfortable uploading the report just to improve a practice answer. **P G**

I3-19 **[Suraj]** How did you handle the result in your own version?

I3-20 **[SUR-3]** Um... I used the number of shifts recovered, which I can explain, and left out the financial saving. I didn't calculate that saving personally. The roommate said it sounded less dramatic but much clearer. Later, another classmate asked why I hadn't escalated sooner. I could answer that without changing the story. That gave me more confidence than making the result sound bigger. **J G**

I3-21 **[Suraj]** How do you keep track of what has improved?

I3-22 **[SUR-3]** Honestly... badly. The spreadsheet has versions but no clear reason for each change. If a senior says shorten it, I shorten. Next person says add context, I add it back. Last time I kept the two-column page and a recording, so at least I can compare. I listened... once. Hearing my own voice is still a little strange. **P**

I3-23 **[Suraj]** What part of your preparation is working well already?

I3-24 **[SUR-3]** Functional questions. Those are... those are okay. I know those examples and the technical follow-ups are useful. I wouldn't want every answer simplified. If the interviewer is an operations leader, the detail matters. My difficulty is... judging how much context this particular listener needs. One standard answer doesn't solve that. **J G Q**

I3-25 **[Suraj]** Anything else before we finish?

I3-26 **[SUR-3]** People say be confident, but I was already confident about the work. I needed somebody to show me where the explanation lost them. That's different. And, uh, don't make my grammar too perfect in your notes. I definitely didn't speak like a brochure. **G Q**

I3-27 **[Suraj]** When you explained that shutdown to your roommate, what did you say instead of the technical words?

I3-28 **[SUR-3]** One term was the name of a machine stage. He didn't need that. I said, this is the step where a small fault can stop the next shift. Then he understood why production didn't want another interruption. I kept the safety check in, though. That wasn't optional background. Without it, my suggestion sounds faster and easier than it was. So... simplifying wasn't removing every technical detail. It was explaining why one detail mattered and dropping another one that didn't affect the decision. **J G**

I3-29 **[Suraj]** Did you save that explanation anywhere?

I3-30 **[SUR-3]** On the paper, in the margin. Not very systematic. I wrote, explain the shift risk first. Then I tried without looking. The first time I still used the machine term. Habit, I suppose. My roommate stopped me, I said it again, and then he could follow. We didn't improve the entire answer that evening. Just that bit. I was fine with that. If I try to fix the opening, the result, the length, everything together, I can't remember what I changed when I speak next. **J G**

I3-31 **[Suraj]** Okay. Thanks, SUR-3, that's helpful.

I3-32 **[SUR-3]** Sure. Send me the notes if you need me to check a detail.
