### **Interview 1: SUR-1 (consulting)**

I1-1 **[Suraj]** Thanks for helping. I'm looking at how classmates prepare for placements. We have about six or seven minutes. Is it okay to record this for the assignment, shared only with the course team?

I1-2 **[SUR-1]** Yeah, that's fine. Just don't put my company name in the write-up, please.

I1-3 **[Suraj]** Sure, yeah. What are you preparing for at the moment?

I1-4 **[SUR-1]** Um... consulting, mainly the MBB firms, plus a couple of strategy roles. Before ISB I was in analytics, four years. Around four, yeah. So cases are taking most of my evenings. Fit questions are happening in the gaps, which is probably not a very good system. **J**

I1-5 **[Suraj]** Take me through your most recent practice session.

I1-6 **[SUR-1]** Uh... Tuesday night, with a friend from my section. We booked thirty minutes, started late, then did a profitability case. That went reasonably okay. At the end he asked about a time I influenced somebody without authority. I used a pricing project. The one I normally use, actually. By the time I reached what I had actually done, he stopped me. Apparently I'd been speaking for almost four minutes. I thought it was two... honestly. **J P**

I1-7 **[Suraj]** Mm. What had you spent those four minutes explaining?

I1-8 **[SUR-1]** The, uh... the client situation, why pricing was complicated, the team structure... all that. I was trying to make him understand why the work mattered. But I kept saying we built this, we analysed that. He asked, okay, but which decision was yours? And I had to... almost restart the story. That was a bit uncomfortable because I know that project properly. I wasn't making anything up. **P**

I1-9 **[Suraj]** What did you do afterwards?

I1-10 **[SUR-1]** Opened my... what do I call it? My story sheet. I have eight examples in a Google document, headings like leadership, failure, conflict. I cut the pricing answer down and recorded it on my phone. The first recording was still over three minutes. Then I removed the whole... the whole explanation of the data pipeline. It felt wrong to remove it, but the story still made sense without it. **J G**

I1-11 **[Suraj]** How much time went into that?

I1-12 **[SUR-1]** Uh, forty... maybe forty-five minutes after the mock. And next morning I listened while walking to class. I'd already spent about an hour preparing that example on Sunday. This week I did two fit sessions; cases were nearly every day. I don't track the time exactly, no, but fit prep keeps eating whatever time is left before sleep. **P**

I1-13 **[Suraj]** How did you judge the shorter answer?

I1-14 **[SUR-1]** Yeah, so I sent the recording to the same friend. He could tell me what I did, which was useful. But he wanted the result in the opening sentence. Another person had told me to build suspense and reveal the result later. So... now I'm deciding whose advice to follow. Both have done consulting internships. Neither advice sounded completely stupid. I mean... both made sense when they said it. **J P G**

I1-15 **[Suraj]** How did you resolve that difference?

I1-16 **[SUR-1]** I didn't fully. I kept the result towards the end but put my decision much earlier. Then I asked a senior to interrupt me with questions. When he asked why I hadn't used a simpler analysis, I could explain the trade-off. That made me more comfortable than someone just saying, nice story. There was something to... yeah, something to test, you know? **J G**

I1-17 **[Suraj]** Have you tried anything else with that answer?

I1-18 **[SUR-1]** ChatGPT. Once... well, for that answer, once. I pasted a version without names and asked what was unclear. It spotted that my role wasn't obvious, fair enough. Then I asked for a rewrite and it became very... polished. Something about orchestrating cross-functional alignment. I have never said orchestrating in my life. Also it made the business impact sound like my individual result. That part I deleted. **J P Q**

I1-19 **[Suraj]** Mm-hm. What did you keep from it?

I1-20 **[SUR-1]** Mostly the... the questions. Who disagreed with you? What did you change? Those helped me remember details. I didn't keep the rewritten paragraph. I'm not against using it, but I have to check every claim. With a friend also, actually. Sometimes they suggest a bigger number and I'm like, no, I can't defend that number. **J G**

I1-21 **[Suraj]** What changed in your next attempt?

I1-22 **[SUR-1]** Yesterday, uh, I used the same story with a different partner. It was about two minutes twenty, and he asked about the stakeholder conversation rather than asking what my role was. That's one attempt, so I don't know if it's solved. But it felt like we finally discussed the interesting part. My wording was different from the recording, and that was okay. **G**

I1-23 **[Suraj]** How is that different from the case preparation you're doing?

I1-24 **[SUR-1]** Cases... cases aren't the same problem for me. There, I need somebody to push on my structure and maths. For fit, it's... I need to stop hiding behind the background of the project. Also, a score out of ten doesn't help much unless I know which sentence caused the issue. Otherwise I'll just keep editing adjectives and feel productive. **J P Q**

I1-25 **[Suraj]** When you cut the background, what did you actually keep?

I1-26 **[SUR-1]** Um... the disagreement, mainly. The sales person thought our recommendation would be difficult to use. Earlier I was explaining the whole analysis before mentioning that. In the shorter version I said what he disagreed with, what I proposed, and what we tried. I still needed a little background. Otherwise it sounds like I just walked in and convinced everybody. Actually, the difficult bit was... I had changed my own recommendation also. He had a valid point about how the team would use it. That part was missing from my first answer. I was trying to look decisive, I think. When the senior asked what changed after that conversation, I remembered it. So the shorter answer wasn't just the long answer with some sentences deleted. **J G**

I1-27 **[Suraj]** Mm. Have you left any answers alone, rather than keeping on rewriting them?

I1-28 **[SUR-1]** Yeah, my introduction. I was changing that also, every other day. Then one friend said, I understand your background; let's move on. Fair enough. I have a few bullet points now. I don't practise the exact opening sentence each time. The failure example is different, though. There I keep picking an incident and then explaining why it wasn't really my fault. Which... probably misses the question. I haven't settled that one. I need to choose something where I can say what I got wrong without spending half the answer defending myself. It's not that I have no examples. I'm just not very comfortable with the one I've chosen yet. **J P G**

I1-29 **[Suraj]** What happens to all these notes after a mock?

I1-30 **[SUR-1]** They're a bit scattered. Some comments are in the document, some are WhatsApp messages. The recording is somewhere in my phone. Last time I opened an older version by mistake and added back a paragraph I'd already removed. I only noticed when my friend said, didn't we discuss this? So now I put a date beside the story and one line saying what I changed. Very basic. I'm not maintaining some big tracker. After a late mock I usually just want to sleep. And sometimes I don't change anything immediately, because the feedback was... I don't know, more about that person's preferred style. I want to remember the suggestion without treating it like an instruction I have to follow. **J P G**

I1-31 **[Suraj]** Thanks. That's our time. Anyone with a different approach I should speak with?

I1-32 **[SUR-1]** My study group has someone who doesn't write answers at all. Ask her. She'll disagree with half of what I said.
