## **SECTION 1.1 - Personas**

## **Persona 1: The peer-led momentum seeker**

This is an ISB PGP student pivoting into consulting or strategy. They practise cases with friends and an LLM but their preparation falls apart whenever the week gets busy.

1. Persona and who it represents:

This persona is drawn from ADD-1, ADD-2 and ADD-3. Each of them comes from a preMBA role close to the one they now want: ADD-1 from [a large Indian conglomerate] CFO office, ADD-2 from e-commerce strategy and ADD-3 from a technical role. All three are using the one-year PGP to pivot into consulting or business strategy.

2. Tagline:

"It's important to keep that muscle active and to not break practice." (ADD-2).

ADD-3 put the same feeling another way: "Few days you just don't want to do it."

3. Key goals and motivations

   - **Functional:** When placements are four to five weeks away and shortlists are still uncertain, they want to run a case every day with someone who pushes them, so that the structure stays fresh for the interview. ADD-2 describes this as keeping "the mental muscle active".

   - **Functional:** When a case lands in an industry they don't know, they want to understand its value chain quickly, so that they can still give real recommendations. ADD-1, ADD-2 and ADD-3 all raised this.

   - **Emotional:** They want to feel ready at least once. ADD-1 doubts she will ever "feel prepared", and ADD-3 rates himself "Not at all ready".

   - **Social:** They do not want to fall behind classmates who practise more regularly. ADD-2 saw that regular practice had "helped them up their game" while she had "fallen behind".

   - **Motivation:** The pivot is the main driver. ADD-1 says the cohort came to ISB "to get some pivot and a role". ADD-3 wants to move from technical work into a role where he makes the decisions, and ADD-1 admits to "the fear that comes with the competition".

## 4. Environment:

They are in Term 5 of the one-year PGP, spending about four hours a day in class and one to two hours on submissions. Two to six hours of preparation get squeezed into whatever slots are free. Placements start in the first week of December, and shortlists are still arriving. Most practice happens in the atrium or a hostel room with friends, and a session usually takes one to three hours for a case each.

## 5. Challenges:

- **Momentum breaks easily.** After a few days off, the frameworks fade and interviewers notice. ADD-2 felt "guilt or regret" afterwards, and ADD-3 finds the repetition "very boring".

- **They practise with the same few people.** Their partners are friends at the same stage, so ADD-3 questions how useful peer feedback is when "everyone is at the same stage". Sessions also take two to three hours. On Nworks, ADD-1 gets a random interviewer each time, which she says does not "allow you to build familiarity".

- **Unfamiliar industries throw them.** Recommendations fall apart outside sectors they know. ADD-1 finds it hard to give a way forward "in an unfamiliar industry", and ADD-2 struggled with the value chain in an airlines case.

- **Structure slips under stress.** ADD-1 says that without a structure she starts to "blabber words", and that she speaks very fast when nervous. ADD-3 can sense "something else missing" in his answers but cannot tell what it is.

- **It is unclear what to prepare for.** They are unsure which industry or company to focus on, and whether to prepare for the shortlists they have or the roles they want. ADD-2 calls her current preparation "broad and generic".

- **Feedback does not carry over.** Advice tends to fit only the case it was given on. For ADD-3, "the difficulty is how to apply to other cases".

## **Persona 2: The self-led feedback seeker**

This is an ISB PGP student with three to six years of experience who treats preparation like a project. What they lack is not practice, but sharp, specific feedback and easy access to a variety of interviewers.

## 1. Persona and who it represents:

This persona is drawn from ADD-4 and ADD-5, who bring three to six years of experience between them. ADD-4 worked in corporate finance, while ADD-5 moved across consulting and product roles over three job changes. ADD-4 is targeting finance (IB, PE, VC) with consulting as a backup, and ADD-5 puts consulting first, followed by product or GenMan. Both want to make this pivot count.

## 2. Tagline:

"I do not want something which is very generic in nature or not providing me insights as to where I was going wrong." (ADD-5). ADD-4 pointed to the same gap from another angle: "there's no platform right now which is built for matching case partners."

3. Key goals and motivations:

- **Functional:** After every mock, they want specific feedback on where they went wrong, covering both their content and how they came across, so that each session clearly improves on the last. ADD-4 wanted feedback on his "soft skills" as well as his answer, and ADD-5 rejects anything "very generic in nature".

- **Functional:** When they need a human interviewer, they want to find a suitable partner quickly without committing two hours in return, so that they hear from a range of interviewers. ADD-4 calls finding a partner "a bottleneck", and ADD-5 wants "unbiased interviews from people… having very different experiences".

- **Functional:** In the interview itself, they want to recall the best answer they have already prepared, so that they do not walk out with "regrets".

- **Emotional:** They want to stay calm and confident despite the workload and the rejections. ADD-4 insists on keeping "your calm", while ADD-5 notices that rejections start to "affect your mindset" once they stack up.

- **Social:** They want to stand out in a strong cohort. ADD-4 believes anyone who wants to stand out "needs to carry a unique selling point".

- **Motivation:** ADD-5 feels the window to pivot is closing after six years of work, and ADD-4 ultimately wants to move into private equity or venture capital.

## 4. Environment:

They follow the same PGP calendar as Persona 1, with about four hours of class and two hours of submissions a day. They set aside three to three and a half hours for preparation, either in one planned block (ADD-4) or through a goal-based daily split (ADD-5). With "24 days to go" to placements (ADD-4), last-minute company assessments regularly break their routine. ADD-5 lost two hours to an unexpected Amazon test. They practise with one regular case buddy, online or offline, and with alums when they can.

## 5. Challenges:

- **Feedback is generic, content-only or subjective.** ADD-4 wanted feedback on his "soft skills", not just his answer. ADD-5 found tool-generated analysis "not very informative". ADD-4 also notes that "the recommendations can be subjective" from one interviewer to the next.

- **Human partners are costly to find.** ADD-4 calls finding a case partner "a bottleneck", and one case each takes about two hours. On Nworks, ADD-5 found the slots "very limited".

- **The AI has to be policed.** ADD-5 tells the AI that it "should not nudge" him or make the case easier.

- **Unfamiliar industries still trip them up.** In a fintech market-sizing case, ADD-4 could not work out "what the actual market was".

- **Pressure gets in the way of recall.** ADD-5 struggles to bring up his best insight in the moment, which leaves him with "regrets". An alum felt one of his strongest stories came across as "very rehearsed". ADD-4 asked his clarifying questions too late and needs to take notes faster.

- **They lean too hard on frameworks.** A peer told ADD-5 he was "jumping to conclusions" by relying too much on frameworks.

## **<u>Problem hypotheses</u>**

- **Problem Hypothesis 1:** The peer-led momentum seekers among ISB PGP students preparing for consulting and strategy interviews are most frustrated about keeping up daily case practice in the weeks before placements, because their practice depends on a small circle of friends at the same stage being free for two to three hours at a time. A

few busy days break the routine, the frameworks fade, and they walk into the next session visibly less confident.

- **Problem Hypothesis 2:** The self-led feedback seekers among ISB PGP students preparing for consulting and finance interviews are most frustrated about getting specific feedback after each mock interview, because peers, Nworks and AI tools give feedback that is generic, limited to content, or tied to a single case. They build their own workarounds, such as strict AI prompts, recorded answers and master answer sheets, yet still cannot see how they come across or exactly where they went wrong.

- **Problem Hypothesis 3:** The case-interview candidates among ISB PGP students, across both personas, are most frustrated about solving cases in industries they do not know, because casebooks and frameworks give them structure but not industry context. Their recommendations fall apart in sectors such as airlines and fintech, and their only fix is reading primers one case at a time.
