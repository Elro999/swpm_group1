# **Assignment 1 — Personas and Problem Hypotheses**

## **Product scenario**

How students at a business school prepare for placement interviews: the tools they use, where the process breaks down, and what would make it better. Five people were interviewed: three ISB PGP students in placement season (SRI-1, SRI-2, SRI-3) and two working professionals preparing for job switches (SRI-4, SRI-5).

## **Section 1.1 — Personas**

The five interviews group into three personas: students whose prep is blocked by **not knowing what to prepare for** , students whose prep is blocked by **not having a reliable way to practise and keep going** , and working professionals whose prep is **squeezed by job applications and generic, non-company-specific material** . Persona names are fictional; each lists the interviews it draws on.

### **Persona 1: Kabir, the Hedged Switcher**

_Draws on: SRI-3, SRI-1_

"Resource-wise, I am not facing any constraint. But where should I focus and put my efforts in some specific area, I am struggling with that."

**Profile:** 28, PGP Co'27. Former chartered accountant who worked with finance teams in the CFO office on planning and cost optimisation. Came to ISB to pivot into strategy, either internal strategy or external consulting.

#### **Key goals and motivations**

- Convert a consulting or strategy offer that completes the career pivot.

- Keep general management and finance roles open as a hedge.

- Spend limited prep time only on roles he is likely to be shortlisted for.

- Manage placement anxiety by controlling what he can: being ready when a shortlist arrives.

#### **Environment operating in**

- November placement season; the shortlisting stage is already locked through CVs and applications.

- Shortlists arrive close to interviews, and several target companies are still "in talks" with the placement team.

- Preparing several formats at once: cases, guesstimates, personal-interview questions, group discussions and finance technicals.

- Peer mock interviews are about to start.

#### **Challenges**

- Can't tell which roles he will be shortlisted for, so effort is spread thin (e.g. GD prep for one company that may never shortlist him).

- A lingering sense of uncertainty and anxiety that eats into free time.

- AI mock interviews drift into deep dives a real interviewer wouldn't, can't read facial reactions, and lack an interviewer's domain depth.

- Finding alumni from the last three or four cohorts who joined a target role means long manual searches on LinkedIn.

#### **Tools used today**

- Consulting casebooks from IIM Ahmedabad, [a top Indian B-school] and FMS; the ISB finance club prep book.

- ChatGPT and Claude as a case partner; friends for live practice.

- Seniors for CV feedback; club resources for personal-interview questions.

- LinkedIn and the ISB alumni website for finding people in target roles.

### **Persona 2: Neel, the Self-Built Prep System**

_Draws on: SRI-2, with SRI-1 on time and accountability_

"Whenever I wasn't able to find a partner, I leveraged Claude."

**Profile:** 28, PGP Co'27. About five years at a travel-tech company before ISB. Targeting a product management role at an MNC.

#### **Key goals and motivations**

- Land a product role in the next one to two months.

- Practise PM interviews consistently, with or without a partner.

- Turn every mock interview into specific fixes for his weak spots.

- Absorb new material (e.g. AI for product interviews) quickly.

- Balance classes, interview prep, gym, runs and friendships.

#### **Environment operating in**

- Fast business-school life with many competing demands.

- Formed a product prep group of three to four people in mid-September; it thinned in October–November as members got consulting and other non-product shortlists.

- Coordinating schedules with three others; prepares alone with AI when no one is free.

#### **Challenges**

- Practice partners drop away once their own priorities shift.

- Waiting for group schedules to align leads to inaction and lost time.

- Wants accountability, but not someone nagging him.

- AI-generated study documents are long and cost time and mental effort to digest.

- Class learnings fade; class notes, interview recordings and his own thinking sit in separate places.

#### **Tools used today**

- A peer product-prep group.

- Claude with a PM-interview skill from Lenny's Newsletter, acting as interviewer.

- • Recordings and transcripts of every mock, kept in a laptop folder and reviewed for weak spots.

- NotebookLM to turn long documents into podcast-style audio or video.

- Phone calendar and time-management apps.

### **Persona 3: Arjun, the Working Switcher**

_Draws on: SRI-5, SRI-4_

"Most of the time used to be wasted on applying for jobs rather than sitting for preparation."

**Profile:** 26–29, three to four years in a specialist role (data engineering; business analysis for commodity trading and risk management software). Looking for a lateral move while still employed.

**Key goals and motivations**

- Move to a better role without pausing his current job.

- Walk into each interview knowing that company's process, products and what it is good at.

- Turn his own projects and domain expertise into strong answers.

- Get the most value out of free or low-cost AI tools.

#### **Environment operating in**

- Prepares part-time, "on and off", around a full-time job.

- Applies through individual company career sites.

- Works in a niche field where domain expertise and personal networks decide outcomes.

- Interview formats differ by company: two or three rounds, with different emphasis (e.g. Python-heavy).

#### **Challenges**

- Each application is a five-to-six-page form; after 15–20, it becomes tiring and eats prep time.

- AI tools ask generic role questions, not the ones a specific company asks.

- Company-specific question patterns are scattered across LinkedIn posts.

- Details of a company's legacy products aren't available to AI tools.

- AI loses context on follow-up questions and sometimes hallucinates.

- Free-tier limits decide which tool he can use for what.

#### **Tools used today**

- ChatGPT for mock interviews with feedback, and for likely questions generated from his CV and the job description.

- Claude for complex problems; Gemini for company research, documentation in Google Docs and everyday phone use.

- LinkedIn for recruiter profiles and posts about recent interview questions.

- Company PDFs attached to AI chats for product context.

- Ex-colleagues for leads on openings.

## **Problem hypotheses**

**Problem Hypothesis 1:** The **career-switching MBA students** hedging across consulting, strategy and general management roles are most frustrated about **focusing their interview prep on the roles they will actually interview for** because **shortlists and company lineups become visible only shortly before interviews, so effort is spread across cases, GDs and finance technicals that may never be used.**

**Problem Hypothesis 2:** The **self-driven MBA students** preparing without a fixed practice partner are most frustrated about **getting realistic mock-interview practice on demand** because **peer partners drift away once their own shortlists arrive, and AI interviewers go off-script, can't read non-verbal cues and lack a real interviewer's domain depth.**

**Problem Hypothesis 3:** The **career switchers, students and professionals alike,** entering an unfamiliar role are most frustrated about **getting first-hand insight from people who have done the role or been through a company's process** because **finding the right alumni, or recent candidates' accounts of an interview, means hours of manual LinkedIn searching, with no view of who is available to help.**

**Problem Hypothesis 4:** The **time-pressed MBA students** juggling classes, fitness and placements are most frustrated about **keeping their prep consistent and absorbing new material** because **they lack accountability for their free time, and AI-generated study material is too long to digest in the time they have.**

**Problem Hypothesis 5:** The **working professionals** interviewing with specific companies are most frustrated about **tailoring their prep to a particular company's interview** because **AI tools ask generic role questions and don't know each company's**

#### **rounds, recent questions or legacy products, so candidates piece this together from LinkedIn posts and company PDFs.**

**Problem Hypothesis 6:** The **working professionals** job-hunting while employed are most frustrated about **applying to many relevant roles** because **every company's careers site needs a separate five-to-six-page application, which becomes exhausting after 15–20 forms and takes time away from preparation.**
